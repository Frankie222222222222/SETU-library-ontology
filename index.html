SETU Campus Library Ontology

This ontology models the main entities, services, and relationships in a campus library system. It represents people, resources, loan transactions, study rooms, and reservations in a structured semantic model that supports reasoning, querying, and reuse.

Project Overview

The ontology was created in Protégé to describe how a university library operates. It captures users, staff, resources, transactions, and facilities through classes, subclasses, properties, individuals, and formal restrictions.

Compared with a traditional database structure, this ontology supports semantic inference, relationship-based retrieval, and future extension into linked data and knowledge graph applications.

The model focuses on clarity, modularity, and realistic library workflows such as borrowing resources, processing loans, and reserving study rooms.

Class Hierarchy

Library Person ├── Member └── Librarian Resource ├── Book └── Journal Loan StudyRoom Reservation

The hierarchy supports inheritance. For example, both Member and Librarian are subclasses of Person, while Book and Journal are subclasses of Resource.

Restrictions and Consistency

  • Member ⊑ borrows some Resource
  • Loan ⊑ loanMadeBy exactly 1 Member
  • Reservation ⊑ reservesRoom exactly 1 StudyRoom
  • Librarian and Member are disjoint classes
  • Book and Journal are disjoint classes
HermiT reasoning was used in Protégé to verify that the ontology is logically consistent and free from contradictions.

Reasoning Highlights

  • Member and Librarian are inferred as subclasses of Person
  • Book and Journal are inferred as subclasses of Resource
  • Restrictions reinforce valid links between loan, reservation, member, and room entities
  • The model supports semantic verification beyond simple data storage
